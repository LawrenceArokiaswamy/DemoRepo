This is a Roman warship... and a city wall is picking it up.
Rewind.
Syracuse, 213 BC. Before dawn, Rome's fleet creeps toward the city walls.
The plan: get so close that the city's catapults... shoot right over their heads. And it works.
Then, above them... something creaks.
A huge beam swings out over the wall... and lowers an iron hand on a chain.
It grabs the ship by the nose.
Behind the wall, the far end of the beam is pushed down, like a seesaw... and the front of the ship rises.
Higher... until it's standing on its stern.
Then... they let go.
Some ships capsized. Most just filled with water.
The genius behind it wasn't a general. He was a mathematician in his seventies: Archimedes.
The Roman commander joked that Archimedes was using his ships to scoop up seawater... His men didn't laugh.
One ancient writer says that whenever a rope or a stick poked over the wall, they screamed...
THERE IT IS!
...and fled.
Rome stopped attacking the walls. It just waited... because of one old man.

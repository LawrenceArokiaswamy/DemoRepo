# FACTS: PSY03 "Napoleon Was Short"
| Claim in film | Source | Wording relied on |
|---|---|---|
| Napoleon was about 5'6"–5'7" (≈ 1.67–1.69 m), average for his time; "5 ft 2" was in French units (the pouce is longer than the English inch) | The Collector, "Was Napoleon Short?"; ScienceABC; Britannica/History.com (via Lenz) | "approximately 5 feet 6½ inches (around 1.69 m)"; the French inch ≈ 1.066 × the English inch. Some historians (David Bell) estimate 5'4"–5'5", so the film says "about five foot six, or seven" |
| British cartoons popularised "tiny Napoleon" | Same sources (James Gillray caricatures) | "British propaganda, particularly caricatures by James Gillray…" The film shows our own generic cartoon, not Gillray's |
| 1977: repeating a statement makes people rate it as more true | Hasher, Goldstein & Toppino (1977), *J. Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior* 16:107–112 | "a significant increase in the validity judgments for the repeated statements" (sessions 2 weeks apart) |
| 2015: knowing the right answer doesn't fully protect you | Fazio, Brashier, Payne & Marsh (2015), *J. Exp. Psychol.: General* 144(5):993–1002 | "illusory truth effects occurred even when participants knew better" |
| The Great Wall is generally not visible to the naked eye from orbit | NASA archived article "China's Wall Less Great in View from Space"; Yang Liwei (2003, Al Jazeera) | "generally isn't visible, at least to the unaided eye in low Earth orbit". Astronaut testimony is mixed, so the film says "NASA says it's generally not" |

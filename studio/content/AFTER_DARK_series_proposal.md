# After Dark (working title): original cinematic horror mini-series proposal

**Status:** proposal only (2026-11-16). Nothing is in production and no credits or money have been spent. Every paid step needs owner approval first.
**Category added to the backlog:** *Original cinematic horror shorts* (IDs AD01–AD05 in `backlog.json`).
**Brand position:** an occasional strand inside the channel, not a pivot. The channel stays a blend of history, science, mysteries, psychology, alternate realities and animated storytelling. After Dark gets its own playlist, title card and look, but it uses the same characters, renderer and narrator quality bar. At most about **1 in 4 uploads** should be horror.

**Creative rules for every episode:**
- **Dread over shock.** The scare is an idea the viewer works out, not a stinger. No gore, no injury on screen. Allow at most **one** loud sound event per episode, and only as a payoff, never a cheap jump scare.
- **The twist reframes everything.** The last line or shot makes the viewer rethink what they just watched, so it is worth re-watching (good for loops and retention).
- **Faces carry the fear.** Every beat needs a readable reaction: a slow realisation, a held breath, eyes moving to the door. This builds on the Episode 3 lesson that every story beat needs a human reaction.
- **Sound tells half the story.** Room tone, a fridge hum that suddenly stops, knocks placed left or right in the stereo field, a voice through a door. Silence is used as a tool.
- **End with a question for the comments** ("Would you open the door?").
- **YouTube-safe:** suitable for viewers aged 13 and over, with no self-harm and no real people or places.
- **Original:** no recreations of existing creepypastas, films or games.

**Series hook (shared world):**
- All five stories happen in one fictional apartment building, **Ashgrove House**.
- In every episode, something happens at **3:17 AM**. Viewers who notice the pattern have a reason to binge the series and to comment theories.
- The finale (AD05) ties the threads together.
- Production benefit: one set is built once and reused across the whole series.

## 1. Five episode concepts

| ID | Title | Premise | Twist (the reframe) | Main fear |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AD01** | **The Message From Tomorrow** | A man gets a text from his own number, from himself: "Don't open the door at 3:17." At 3:17 there is a knock, and the voice outside is his own. | The voice outside says it sent the warning to keep *the other one* out, and that the other one is already inside. Then a second message from "Me" says the opposite. Two versions of him, and he has to choose which one to trust. | Distrusting yourself |
| **AD02** | **The Neighbours Can Hear You** | A new tenant hears a family talking through the wall every night. Then he realises they are describing what he is doing, as he does it: "He's putting the kettle on." | He records the wall to prove it to the building manager. On playback there are no other voices, only his own, answering them. 4B has been empty for years. | Your own mind |
| **AD03** | **Missing: Tomorrow** | A young woman finds a missing-person poster of herself in the lobby, dated tomorrow. Neighbours keep saying "You look just like her." | She tears it down. Underneath is a second poster, dated today, showing the outfit she is wearing right now. The last line on it reads: "Last seen at Ashgrove House, 3:17 AM." | Time running out |
| **AD04** | **The Voice Log** | His smart speaker's history shows commands every night at 3:17, in his voice: "Is he asleep yet?" "Keep the lights off." | While he reads the log, a new entry appears live: "3:17: He's reading the log. Turn off the lights." The lights go off. | **AD05** | **The Thirteenth Floor** (finale) | Ashgrove House has 12 floors. ## 2. Recommended publishing order

1. **AD01 The Message From Tomorrow (pilot).** It has the strongest hook (a text from yourself, then your own voice at the door), uses the fewest assets (one flat and one phone), and sets up the 3:17 mythology.
2. **AD02 The Neighbours Can Hear You.** Same flat, no new set; mostly driven by sound and the cheapest to make. It tests whether a quieter story driven by audio holds attention.
3. **AD04 The Voice Log.** Same flat plus a speaker prop. It reuses the phone and search-history UI from Episode 3, a natural bridge to the channel's privacy and psychology themes.
4. **AD03 Missing: Tomorrow.** It adds the lobby and a second main character, broadening the series beyond one protagonist.
5. **AD05 The Thirteenth Floor (finale).** It needs the lift set and pays off the series loop, so publish it only once episodes 1–4 have built an audience for it.

**Cadence:** space After Dark episodes between regular episodes (for example history → science → **After Dark** → mystery → …), never back to back.

**Measurement gate:** continue past AD01 only if its average view duration and percentage viewed are at least the channel median; this decision is the owner's. If AD01 underperforms, AD02–AD05 stay in the backlog. They are never removed.

## 3. AD01 "The Message From Tomorrow": 45–60 s story outline (target about 52 s)

**Cast:** Theo, a C+ adult man in a grey hoodie, alone in his flat. **Voices:** the narrator (sparse); Theo's own voice through the door (a second, muffled read of the same voice).

| # | Time | Picture | Sound and words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 s | **Hook.** A dark bedroom. The phone lights up Theo's face. Insert: a message bubble from **"Me"**, with his own face as the contact photo: "Don't open the door at 3:17." Clock reads 3:09. | Phone buzz in silence. Narrator: "At 3:09 AM, Theo got a text… from himself." |
| 2 | 4–10 s | He frowns and checks the sender: it is his own number. Typing dots appear, then: "Whatever it says. Don't." | Narrator: "His number. His photo. And he was still typing." |
| 3 | 10–17 s | He sits on the sofa facing the front door down a short hall, phone in hand. Slow push-in. Clock 3:16. The fridge light behind him clicks off. | The fridge hum **stops**, leaving room tone only. No narration; let it breathe. |
| 4 | 17–23 s | **3:17.** Three slow knocks. His head snaps to the door. The light under the door is broken by the shadow of two feet. | Knocks placed hard left. Through the door, *Theo's own voice*: "Theo. It's me. Open up." |
| 5 | 23–31 s | Close-up as he recognises the voice. He backs away one step. The doorknob turns slowly; the door is locked and holds. | Voice: "I sent you that text. I know what it said." Pause. "I sent it so you wouldn't let **him** in." |
| 6 | 31–37 s | His eyes widen. The voice drops to a whisper and the camera drifts toward the dark bedroom doorway behind him. | Voice: "He's already inside." |
| 7 | 37–44 s | The phone buzzes in his hand. Insert: a new message from **"Me"**: "Don't listen. That's not me. I'm the one in here with you." | One soft floorboard creak from the bedroom, centred behind the viewer. That is the only "loud" moment, and it isn't loud. |
| 8 | 44–50 s | He is frozen between the door and the dark doorway, lit only by the phone. Both threats are off-screen. He does not turn around. | Narrator, quietly: "Two messages. Both from him." |
| 9 | 50–53 s | Insert: a third bubble is typing… "Which one of us do you trust?" Cut to black. One knock. | Title card: **AFTER DARK**, with "3:17 AM" in small type below. |
| End | | Pinned-comment prompt: "Would you open the door?" | |

**Why it works:**
- It poses a question in the first second, and the answer creates a dilemma, not a gore payoff.
- The scares come from sound (the hum stopping, his own voice, one creak) and from his face.
- The ending invites theories in the comments, and the "3:17" title card starts the series pattern.

## 5. Proposed After Dark visual style (existing renderer)

Same characters and renderer, a different mood. The aim is recognisably the same channel with a different time of night.

- **Light:**
  - Key light: cold moonlight from one window, blue (`#3a4a7a`), low intensity.
  - A **single warm practical** per shot: the phone screen, the gap under the door, or a fridge light. Faces are lit mainly by the phone, an extension of the Episode 3 phone-glow work.
  - Shadows sit close to black, with no fill light in wide shots. Close-ups get a very faint cold fill (`S.fill` at about 0.3) so expressions still read.
- **Grade:**
  - Desaturated, at about 60% colour.
  - One accent colour per episode (phone cyan in AD01).
  - Heavy vignette, slightly raised painted noise (`paintK` 0.06 → about 0.09) that reads as film grain, and low ground fog in the hall.
- **Camera:**
  - Locked-off frames and slow push-ins (2–4% per second). No shaky cam.
  - Use **negative space**: frame the protagonist off-centre so an empty doorway or a dark hall fills the rest of the frame, and the viewer keeps checking it.
  - The "no static hold over 2 s" rule still applies, achieved through micro-motion: breathing, a slow push, a flicker.
- **Characters:**
  - The same C+ v2 cast and rig.
  - Fear expressions built from existing parameters: wide `open`, raised `brow`, tight `mouth`, eyes darting with gaze control, a swallow (the Episode 3 gulp).
  - No redesign is needed.
- **Text:**
  - Captions are smaller, in white, with no yellow highlight except one key word.
  - The title card is **AFTER DARK** in thin, widely spaced capitals, with "3:17 AM" beneath.
  - The thumbnail shares the brand system: one face lit by a phone, one dark doorway, at most 2–3 words.
- **Sound:**
  - Mixed quieter overall, with room tone always present.
  - Silence and cut-offs are used as events.
  - Off-screen sources are placed left or right in the stereo field.
  - No sudden loud stingers.

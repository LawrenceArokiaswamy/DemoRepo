## 1. Vasa: "The Warship That Sank in Twenty Minutes" (EP02), RANK 1

### 1.1 Hook (0–2 s)
- **Visual:** low hero angle on a gold-carved warship under full sail, a crowd cheering on the shore, cannons run out.
- **Hard cut** to our C+ sailor on deck, mid-wave to the crowd. The horizon behind him **slowly tilts**, and his smile freezes.
- **Narration:** "This was the mightiest warship in Sweden. It lasted twenty minutes."
- **Mute test:** the tilt plus the frozen smile read as WHAT?! without words.

### 1.2 Story outline (target 44–48 s, ≈110–120 words)

| Time | Beat | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 s | **Hook** | As above. |
| 2–8 s | **Who / why** | Stockholm, 1628. King Gustav II Adolf wants the biggest, most heavily armed ship in the Baltic: two full gun decks, 64 cannons, hundreds of gilded carvings. *Picture:* the royal letter; the cannons multiplying along the hull. |
| 8–17 s | **The test (OH NO setup, humour)** | Weeks before the voyage, thirty sailors run from one side of the deck to the other. The ship rolls further with every run. After three runs, the admiral stops the test. *Picture:* C+ sailors sprinting back and forth, getting dizzier; the deck tilting more each time; the admiral's face. Then: **"They sailed anyway."** Beat of silence; sailors exchange glances; nobody wants to tell the king. |
| 17–30 s | **Maiden voyage (suspense, WOW)** | 10 August 1628. A salute fires, the crowd cheers, and a light gust heels her over. She recovers. A second gust. *Picture:* on the lower gun deck, our sailor watches water come in through the **open gunports**. Silence before the plunge. She sinks about 1,300 m from where she started, in front of the whole city. |
| 30–36 s | **Who's to blame? (humour)** | An inquiry. The shipwright had died; the king had approved the design; nobody is punished. *Picture:* a circle of C+ officials all pointing at each other. |
| 36–48 s | **WHOA ending** | 333 years in the cold, brackish Baltic, where shipworms can't survive. In 1961 she rises from the harbour almost intact. **Twist:** archaeologists found four of the builders' rulers. Two measured in Swedish feet (12 inches); two in Amsterdam feet (11 inches). One side of the ship is heavier than the other. *Final line (labelled as a theory):* "Sweden's mightiest warship… may have been sunk by a ruler." The C+ shipwright looks at his ruler, then at camera. |

### 1.3 Central curiosity
How does the most powerful warship of its day sink in calm water, on its first trip, in front of everyone? Second question: why did they launch it after it failed the test?

### 1.4 Emotional progression
- **WHAT?!** A grand ship tilting over a cheering crowd.
- **Humour:** the sailors' running test and the "launched it anyway" glance, which is natural irony, not a forced joke.
- **OH NO!** Gusts, then water at the gunports, then silence.
- **Humour (light):** the blame circle. This is safe because it is about the institution, not the victims.
- **WHOA!** The ship rising after 333 years, then the ruler twist.
- **Tone guard:** about 30 people died. No jokes during the sinking; the death toll is stated respectfully.

### 1.5 Ending / twist
The ruler theory. It is surprising, visual and shareable ("a units mistake sank a warship"), and it echoes modern unit disasters. It must be framed as **one theory**, and the narration must not say it is proven.

### 1.6 Sources and uncertainty
- **Stability test** **[VERIFIED-WEB]:** 30 men ran across the deck; the test was stopped after three runs because the ship rolled alarmingly. Vice Admiral Klas Fleming still let the voyage go ahead.
  - [Vasa Museum audio guide: the inquiry](https://www.vasamuseet.se/besok/audioguide/audioguide-english/textversion/8.-the-inquiry), [Irish Times (2023)](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/europe/2023/05/16/the-vasa-was-one-of-the-most-powerful-ships-in-the-world-for-about-20-minutes), [Military History Matters](https://military-history.org/articles/back-to-the-drawing-board-vasa-2.htm).
- **Distance before sinking** **[SOURCE-TO-CHECK]:** 1,300 m is the common figure; one source says ≈1,400 yards (≈1,280 m). Script wording: "barely a kilometre".
- **Raised in April 1961; wood preserved by the cold, low-oxygen Baltic water** **[VERIFIED-WEB]:** about **95%** original wood (the "98%" sometimes quoted was not confirmed); the museum opened in 1990.
  - [Vasa Museum: salvage](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/vasa-history/salvage), [Archaeology Magazine](https://archive.archaeology.org/1207/features/vasa_warship_sweden_stockholm_harbor.html).
- **Death toll** **[UNCERTAIN]:** commonly "about 30" (some sources say up to 50); remains of at least 15 people were found. Script wording: "about thirty people died".
- **Water through open lower gunports** **[SOURCE-TO-CHECK]:** standard account (Wikipedia; museum texts). Confirm on vasamuseet.se before the script is locked.
- **Ruler / asymmetry theory** **[UNCERTAIN]:** Vasa Museum archaeologist Fred Hocker measured the hull. He reported more structure on the port side and four rulers in two different units (Swedish and Amsterdam feet). He presents this as a **possible contributor**; the main causes are top-heaviness and too little stability.
  - [The World (PRI, 2013)](https://theworld.org/stories/2013/08/15/swedish-preservationists-document-likely-cause-sinking-ancient-sailing-ship), [WGBH (2016)](https://www.wgbh.org/news/2016-10-11/new-clues-emerge-in-centuries-old-swedish-shipwreck).
  - The book: Hocker, *Vasa: A Swedish Warship* (2011).
  - **Must be labelled "one theory".**
- **"Most-visited museum in Scandinavia"** **[SOURCE-TO-CHECK]:** not used in the outline.
- **Labels on screen:** the king's letter and the blame circle are **DRAMATIZATION**; the gun-deck interior is a **RECONSTRUCTION**.

### 1.7 Character acting
- **Stability test:** sprinting, wobbling and dizzy C+ sailors. We have springs and squash; foot planting is the carry-forward item, and a run is the place to prove it.
- **The admiral:** hand-on-helmet worry ("If only His Majesty were here"). The quote is **[UNCERTAIN]**, so we won't put it in a speech bubble.
- **The sailor below decks:** blink, glance, surprise, pointing at the gunport. This is exactly the acting test, reused.
- **The blame circle:** everyone points at someone else.
- **Shipwright's final look:** ruler, then a sheepish look to camera.

### 1.8 Cinematic 3D shots
- Low crane up the gilded stern; the crowd and city in the background.
- Deck-level Dutch angle as the horizon rolls.
- Gun-deck interior, with water bursting through the ports, backlit.
- Underwater: the hull settling into murk, then a time-lapse of 333 years.
- The rise: the ship breaking the surface (1961), slow push-in.
- Macro: the two rulers side by side, with the 11-inch and 12-inch marks offset.

### 1.9 Reusable assets
- `ocean.js` (waves, wakes, splashes).
- Painterly style plus the `golden` / `day` presets.
- The spline camera, the ship-ride camera (`frameOf` attach), the painted shadow cards.
- **C+ character system** (rig, face, springs, acting test performance).
- 45 CC0 props (crates, barrels, buckets, lanterns), the fleet logic for background boats, the SFX library and mix pipeline.

## 2. Hero's temple doors: "The Doors That Opened Themselves" (EP05), RANK 2

### 2.1 Hook (0–2 s)
- **Visual:** a dark temple. A priest lights the altar fire, and two huge bronze doors **swing open by themselves**. A C+ worshipper's jaw drops.
- **Narration:** "Nobody touched these doors. The fire opened them."

### 2.2 Story outline (target 38–42 s)

| Time | Beat | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 s | **Hook** | As above. |
| 2–9 s | **Who** | Alexandria, about 2,000 years ago. Worshippers think the god has answered. In the shadows, the inventor Hero (C+ inventor variant) smiles. |
| 9–24 s | **X-ray dive** | The camera sinks through the floor, one action per line: the fire heats the air in a sealed altar → the air pushes water out of a hidden globe → a bucket fills and gets heavy → it drops and pulls ropes → the ropes turn the door posts. |
| 24–32 s | **OH NO (humour)** | When the fire dies, the water flows back and the doors close themselves. A late worshipper runs up just as they shut in his face. |
| 32–40 s | **WHOA ending** | Hero wrote this down nearly 2,000 years ago: heat turned into motion. Final line: "The world's first automatic door ran on fire." It could end with a modern sliding door as a callback. |

### 2.3 Central curiosity
"How do doors open with no one touching them, 2,000 years before electricity?"

### 2.4 Emotional progression
Awe → suspicion ("it's a trick") → wonder at the hidden machine → laugh (the late worshipper) → admiration.

There is no danger, so the suspense is "how?", not "will they survive?". This lowers its ceiling compared with Vasa.

### 2.5 Ending
The "first automatic door" line, with a modern-door callback.

### 2.6 Sources and uncertainty
- Hero of Alexandria, *Pneumatica*, "Temple doors opened by fire on an altar" **[VERIFIED-WEB]**.
  - Mechanism: heated air → water forced into a hanging vessel → its weight turns the door spindles via ropes; a counterweight closes them as the fire cools and the water returns.
  - [Pneumatics, Woodcroft translation, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/etext/77400); [Sparavigna, "Water, air and fire at work in Hero's machines" (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.3470).
- **[UNCERTAIN]:** no remains have ever been found, and it is not known whether any temple installed it.
  - Wording: "Hero **described** a temple where…".
  - The temple scene is labelled **RECONSTRUCTION**.
- The **dating** "1st century AD" is the common estimate.
- **Timing:** opening speed was probably slow (minutes or more). Our compressed timing is labelled as dramatized.

### 2.7 Character acting
- The priest's theatrical gesture.
- Worshippers' awe (surprised, then happy).
- Hero's knowing smirk (suspicious expression).
- The late worshipper's run-up and door-in-face frustration, then a sheepish look to camera.

### 2.8 Cinematic shots
- Firelit interior with volumetric-looking god rays (painted cards).
- Doors opening toward the camera.
- A continuous "X-ray" crane down through the floor into the machine room.
- Macro on rope drums turning; the fire dying.

### 2.9 Reuse
- Syracuse temple, marble and sandstone materials.
- C+ system; the **inventor costume is already designed** (C+ concept sheet).
- Lantern, candlestick and vase props.
- Workshop set (Hero's study).

### 2.10 New assets and effort (≈3.5–4 days)

| Asset | Effort |
|---|---|
| Temple interior + doors | 1 day |
| Mechanism cutaway (globe, pipes, bucket, ropes, drums) | 1 day |
| Fire effect | 0.25 day |
| C+ priest and inventor variants | 0.5 day |
| Animatic → final | 1.5 days |

## 3. The Mechanical Turk: "The Chess Robot That Fooled the World" (EP07), RANK 3

### 3.1 Hook (0–2 s)
- **Visual:** candlelit hall. A robed automaton turns its head and **sweeps every chess piece off the board**. Napoleon (C+ variant) stares.
- **Narration:** "Napoleon tried to cheat at chess… against a robot."
- The anecdote is **[UNCERTAIN]**, so it is worded as "the story goes" or labelled.

### 3.2 Story outline (target 42–48 s)

| Time | Beat | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 s | **Hook** | As above. |
| 2–10 s | **Who** | Vienna, 1770. Wolfgang von Kempelen unveils a chess-playing machine for Empress Maria Theresa. He opens every door to show the gears; a candle shines straight through. |
| 10–22 s | **Escalation** | It beats almost everyone and tours Europe and America for decades. In 1809, the story goes, Napoleon makes an illegal move. The Turk puts the piece back. He cheats again, and the Turk sweeps the board. |
| 22–36 s | **WHOA reveal** | Cutaway: a chess master is hidden inside, sliding his seat as each door is opened. He follows the game through magnets under the board, by candlelight, and moves the arm with a lever. |
| 36–46 s | **Twist ending** | Destroyed by fire in 1854. Today Amazon's "Mechanical Turk" is named after it: a service where hidden humans do work that looks automated. |

### 3.3 Central curiosity
"How could a machine from 1770 beat people at chess?"

### 3.4 Emotional progression
Wonder → comic confrontation (Napoleon) → the satisfying "trick revealed" → a modern twist that lands on today.

### 3.5 Ending
The modern namesake. It is strong and shareable.

### 3.6 Sources and uncertainty
- Built 1770 by von Kempelen; toured by Maelzel after Kempelen's death; destroyed in a fire at Philadelphia's Chinese Museum, 5 July 1854 **[VERIFIED-WEB]**.
  - [Wikipedia (summary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_Turk), [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/how-a-phony-18th-century-chess-robot-fooled-the-world), [Heinz Nixdorf MuseumsForum](https://hnf.de/en/permanent-exhibition/exhibition-areas/the-mechanization-of-information-technology/early-automatons-miracles-of-technology/wolfgang-von-kempelens-chess-turk.html).
  - Books: Tom Standage, *The Turk* (2002); Gerald M. Levitt, *The Turk, Chess Automaton* (2000).
- **Napoleon game** **[UNCERTAIN]:** sources disagree on the details (number of illegal moves, the board sweep). It needs "as the story goes".
- **Franklin game** **[UNCERTAIN]:** not used.
- **The Amazon service's name and its link to the Turk** **[SOURCE-TO-CHECK]** (Amazon's own description).
- **Sensitivity:** the automaton wore Ottoman-style costume (the period "Turk" exoticism). Show it as a historical machine figure without caricature, and keep the joke on Napoleon and the hoax.

### 3.7 Character acting
- Napoleon's cheating, then indignation.
- Kempelen's showman flourishes.
- The hidden operator, cramped, sweating and holding a candle (a comic and sympathetic close-up).
- The empress's delight.

### 3.8 Cinematic shots

- The "doors open, candle shines through" trick shot.
- A cutaway slide revealing the hidden man.
- Magnet macro under the board.

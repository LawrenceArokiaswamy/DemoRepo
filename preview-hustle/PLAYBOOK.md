# Website Preview Hustle: Milton / Halton electricians

Goal: $4,000 CAD a month, built from setup fees plus monthly care plans.

## 1. Niche: electricians first, HVAC in February

**Why electricians:**
- **Timing.** In Nov–Feb, HVAC companies are flat out with furnace calls and plumbers with frozen pipes. They won't read your email. Electricians have a quieter winter and want leads lined up for spring.
- **Job value.** Panel upgrades ($3–6K), EV charger installs ($1.5–3K), basement finishing and pot lights. One extra job pays for the website, so $1,500 is an easy yes.
- **Lots of owner-operators.** These are 1–10 person shops that often have a 2015-era Wix or GoDaddy site, or none at all.
- **A built-in trust signal.** In Ontario, the ESA/ECRA licence number matters to homeowners. Many old sites bury it or leave it out. Your preview puts it at the top.

**Later:** add HVAC in Feb–Mar, before AC season, using the same template and new copy. Then plumbers.

**Territory.** Milton alone is too small for 100+ previews a month. Work all of Halton plus nearby towns: Milton, Halton Hills (Georgetown, Acton), Oakville, Burlington, Campbellville, Mississauga west, Guelph and Brampton.

## 3. Who will actually pay (the qualifying filter)

Only build previews for businesses that pass **all** of these:
- [ ] **Active:** at least one Google review in the last 3 months, or recent posts.
- [ ] **Established:** 15–150 Google reviews. Under 15 is often too small to pay. Well over 150 usually means they already have an agency.
- [ ] **Has a site that's clearly weak:** looks bad on a phone, no click-to-call, no quote form, copyright 2019 or older, no HTTPS, or broken pages.
- [ ] **Not a franchise** (Mr. Electric and similar have corporate sites).
- [ ] **Not already agency-built:** skip sites with many "Electrician in [City]" landing pages or a "Website by ___" credit.
- [ ] **Bonus signals of willingness to pay:** they run Google ads, pay for HomeStars or Yelp listings, or show truck wraps in photos. These businesses already spend on marketing.

**How to find them:** search Google Maps for "electrician Milton ON" and **skip the top 3 results** (they have SEO already). Scroll down and open businesses with 15–150 reviews whose listing links to a website. Check each site **on your phone**: that's what the owner's customers see. Repeat for each town.

## 4. 2-minute phone audit (fill in the CSV)

Score each 1–10 (10 = worst):
1. **Mobile:** do you have to pinch or zoom? Is text tiny? Does the menu break?
2. **Speed:** is it still loading after 3 seconds?
3. **Call to action:** is there a "Call" or "Get a quote" button in the first screen?
4. **Booking or quote form:** Y/N
5. **Looks dated:** stock photos, old fonts, 2015-era design, old copyright year

TOTAL = sum. Build previews for the highest totals that pass the filter in section 3.

## 5. Contact rules (Canada) — not legal advice

**Email is covered by CASL.** Cold email to a business is allowed only under implied consent. The usual basis is *conspicuous publication*:
- The business published the address itself, on its own website. Don't use an address taken from a third-party directory.
- There's no "no solicitation" notice near it.
- Your message relates to their business role. A pitch about their company website to the owner fits this.

Every email must include your name, business name, **mailing address**, and a working unsubscribe. Honour opt-outs within 10 business days. Keep a record of where you found each address (the `source_note` column).

**Phone is the cleanest route.** Calls business-to-business are generally exempt from the National Do Not Call List rules. Call, say who you are, and offer to text or email the preview link. Once they say yes, you have express consent.

**Contact forms:** fine if you clearly identify yourself. Never make it look like a customer enquiry.

**Never invent** reviews, years in business, licence numbers or service areas. Use only what's on their current site or Google profile. Leave gaps blank.

## 6. Outreach scripts

**Call (30 seconds):**
> "Hi, is this [owner]? My name's [you], I'm a web designer here in Milton. I was looking at [business]'s website on my phone and put together a modern version for you, already done, no cost to look. Can I text you the link? ... Great. If you like it, it's $[price] and it's yours. If not, no worries, I'll take it down Friday."

Subject: New website for [Business] (already built)

[Your name] · [Business name] · [phone]

**Follow-up day 7:** "Last note from me. The preview comes down Friday. Want me to keep it up?"

## 7. Delivery checklist (have this ready before the first "yes")

- [ ] Simple 1-page agreement: scope, price, care-plan terms, cancel anytime, who owns the domain (them)
- [ ] Payment: Stripe or Square payment link; deposit of 50% or the full amount upfront
- [ ] Get access to their domain registrar, or transfer the domain. Point DNS to your host (Cloudflare Pages / Netlify)
- [ ] Remove `noindex`, add Google Analytics or Plausible, and connect the Google Business Profile website link
- [ ] Register a business name in Ontario (sole proprietorship, about $60). Get an HST number once revenue passes $30K a year

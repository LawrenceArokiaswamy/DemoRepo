# Facts: The Map Nobody Believed

- Alfred Wegener (1880-1930) was a German meteorologist (weather scientist).
- January 1912: he presented continental drift; his book followed in 1915.
- Evidence: the fit of South America and Africa, the same fossils on both sides of the Atlantic (Mesosaurus, Glossopteris) and matching rock layers.
- He could not name a force strong enough to move continents; most geologists rejected and mocked the idea (for example at the 1926 New York symposium).
- November 1930: he died on the Greenland ice sheet during an expedition.
- 1950s: ocean-floor mapping (Marie Tharp and Bruce Heezen) showed the Mid-Atlantic Ridge with a rift valley down its middle.
- 1960s: seafloor spreading and plate tectonics proved the continents move. Magma rises at mid-ocean ridges and makes new sea floor that spreads outward.
- The Atlantic widens about 2.5 cm a year, roughly the speed fingernails grow.

Dramatized: "saw a jigsaw puzzle" (he noticed the fit on a map); the map shapes are simplified cartoons.

# Facts: Sprouting Cows

- Edward Jenner (1749-1823) was a country doctor in Berkeley, Gloucestershire.
- Dairy workers had long said that catching cowpox protected you from smallpox.
- 14 May 1796: Jenner took matter from cowpox sores on the hand of milkmaid Sarah Nelmes and put it into small cuts on the arm of James Phipps, aged 8.
- 1 July 1796 (about seven weeks later): he inoculated the boy with smallpox matter. James did not fall ill.
- Smallpox killed about 3 in 10 of the people it infected.
- The Royal Society did not accept his 1797 paper; he published "An Inquiry into the Causes and Effects of the Variolae Vaccinae" himself in 1798.
- 1802: James Gillray's cartoon "The Cow-Pock" showed vaccinated people sprouting little cows.
- 1980: the World Health Organization declared smallpox eradicated.
- "Vaccine" and "vaccination" come from variolae vaccinae, "smallpox of the cow" (Latin vacca = cow).

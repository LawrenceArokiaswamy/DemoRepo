# Facts: The Clock That Saved Sailors

- John Harrison (1693-1776), a carpenter and self-taught clockmaker from Lincolnshire, began his sea clocks around 1730.
- Scilly naval disaster, 22 October 1707: four Royal Navy ships lost; estimates of 1,300 to 2,000 men died. It is often blamed on poor longitude, which led to the Longitude Act of 1714.
- H1 to H3 took about 1730-1759 (H3 alone about 19 years). H4 (1759) is a large pocket watch.
- On the 1761-62 trial to Jamaica (81 days at sea) H4 lost about 5 seconds.
- The Earth turns 360° in 24 hours = 15° an hour, so the time difference between home and where you are gives your longitude.
- The Board of Longitude held back the prize; George III backed Harrison and Parliament paid him in 1773.
- GPS receivers work out position from the timing of signals from satellites that carry atomic clocks.

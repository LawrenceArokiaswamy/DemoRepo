# The formula: "they laughed… then science proved them right"

Viral science-story Shorts are not facts. They are tiny stories with a hidden science twist, built on six beats.

## The six beats
| # | Beat | What happens | Example (the clock story) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Weird action** | Someone does something strange you can show in one picture, in the first line. No setup. | A carpenter says he can save the navy… with a clock. |
| 2 | **The laugh** | People mock it, doubt it, ban it or call it crazy. (Stakes go here too: one number.) | Four warships hit the rocks; the experts laugh: "A clock? On a ship?" |
| 3 | **They look right** | A moment where the doubters seem correct: it fails, it's slow, it's mocked again. | Pendulums stop in one big wave. His third clock takes 19 years. |
| 4 | **The clue** | One small detail that hints the hero is right. | The fourth clock fits in his hand. |
| 5 | **The payoff** | Proof: a result, a number or a disaster. | 81 days at sea… it lost 5 seconds. |
| 6 | **The science** | The real mechanism in 1-2 plain sentences. | The Earth turns 15° every hour, so time = position. |
| + | **Last line** | Short, surprising, a little arguable, so people comment. | "GPS… is just very good clocks." |

## What makes a story work
- One picture tells the weird action (a bell on a grave, gaps in rails, chlorine by a door, a clock on a ship).
- Real doubt (check it: many "everyone laughed" tales are myths; "doubted" or "ignored" is fine).
- A number for the stakes and a number for the payoff.
- Science a 12-year-old gets in two sentences.
- A last line people want to argue about.
Story types to mix: A laughed-at genius · B weird old practice with a real reason · C "imagine you…" situations · D a famous person's strange habit.
Skip: anything that needs gore, living private people, politics, and stories other channels already made (check YouTube).

## Script rules
- 140-175 words, 13-20 short lines, ~50-62 seconds. One idea per line, spoken US English, contractions.
- Write numbers as words ("seventeen thirty"); the captions turn them back into digits with `captions.subs`.
- "…" = a pause before a punchline. Put 1-3 `*star*` words per line (yellow captions, never spoken).
- Every line must be something you can SHOW. Cut lines that only explain.
- First line = the weird action. Last line = the arguable twist, and nothing after it.

## The look and the edit (built into the engine)
Simple round-head characters (`style: "round"`), rich detailed places (`Wd.room`, sea, ships, streets, space), a new shot every 2-3 s (`CS.shots`), one-word captions centred low (`captions`), a sound on every cut (`plan.cutSounds`), crowd laughs on the laugh (`"laugh"`), a title card for time jumps (`CS.title("19 YEARS")`).

## Titles that work (pick 3 for the upload)
- "Why ___ ___" → Why Sailors Trusted a Clock
- "The ___ Nobody Believed" → The Clock Nobody Believed
- "What ___ Really ___" → What Saved the Navy
- "They Laughed at ___ Until ___" → They Laughed at a Carpenter Until 1761
Keep titles under 50 characters and never promise what the video doesn't show.

## Make it yours (monetization)
YouTube doesn't pay for reused or mass-produced content. Pick your own stories, give your hero a signature look (a colour, a hat) and keep it, keep the facts right, and use one voice for the channel (your own voice is best).

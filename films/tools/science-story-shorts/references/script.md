# The script

Narration only: no stage directions in the spoken text. Size it to the length:
| Length | Words (narrator pace, ~2.5-3 words/s) |
|---|---|
| 20 s | ~55 |
| 30 s | ~80 |
| 45 s | ~120 |
| 60 s | ~160 |

## The shape (it works for almost every story)
1. **Hook** (sentence 1, no setup): the surprising fact. "Two empires almost went to war… over one pig."
2. **Setting** in 1-2 short lines: when, where, who, one concrete detail. "Eighteen fifty-nine. A tiny island between America and Canada."
3. **Escalation**, 2-4 beats, each bigger than the last. Someone doubts or overreacts. One exaggeration joke.
4. **Pivot**: "But…", "So…", "Then…" brings in the real turn.
5. **Twist / payoff**: short, ironic, and the last words of the video. "The only casualty of the Pig War… was the pig."

## Style
- Plain spoken English, contractions, sentences of 4-14 words. Write numbers as words ("nineteen thirty-two"), so the timing matches the voice.
- Every sentence must be something you can show. Cut lines that only explain.
- Use "…" where the narrator should pause before a punchline. Those pauses are where the picture does its jokes.
- Never describe what the viewer already sees.

## Reply with
- The script in a copyable block, the word count and the estimated length.
- A short visual plan: one line per shot.
- Recording tips:
  - One steady, warm storyteller voice. ElevenLabs works well (a deep narrator or documentary voice, stability ~40-55 %). Any AI voice works, and so does your own voice recorded on a phone in a quiet room.
  - Leave natural pauses at the "…" and at sentence ends. No music in the file.
  - MP3, WAV or M4A are all fine.
  - If you change any words while recording, send the final text too.
- The ask: **"Upload the voiceover when it's ready and I'll animate it."**

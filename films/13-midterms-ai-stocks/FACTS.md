# FACTS: "America just voted. AI stocks just fell. Connected?"

Researched November 7, 2026, four days after the midterm vote on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. Every number on screen or in the voice is listed here with its source and date. Numbers are rounded simply (5.52% → "five and a half percent", 12.25% → "twelve percent").

**How the research was done:** web search only. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (cnbc.com, nbcnews.com and axios.com did not resolve), so each figure comes from search results quoting the outlet, plus syndicated wire copies. Where outlets disagreed, the conservative or later (closing) figure is used, and the disagreement is noted. **Before posting, a human should open the links in sections 1–3 and confirm the numbers.**

## 3. Why, in kid terms: what the reporting says (the heart of the film)

Every reason is something major outlets reported this week, presented as "investors fear…" or "worries", never as proven cause.

**Reason 1: data centers might slow (the election-linked reason).**
- Reuters (Nov 5): the selloff followed Democrats' midterm victory, "including in some key data-center states", which "stoked concern that AI data-center buildout could slow". Investors also feared tougher AI regulation under a Democratic-led Congress.
- CNBC, "AI stocks hammered by midterm shock as data center buildout becomes a 'war of attrition'" (Nov 6). Coverage cited UBS: if construction pauses spread, nearly a third of the new US data-center capacity expected in 2028 could be delayed or lost. (Not used on screen.)
- Voice: "Investors fear the election could slow building them. Fewer data centers, fewer chips sold." The second sentence is the plain-language link: chipmakers sell to data centers, and chip stocks fell most (−12%). Comparison: a brick maker hearing the town might stop building new houses.

**Reason 2: big AI spending, profits look slower (started before the vote).**
- Bloomberg, "Big Tech Selloff Pushes Nasdaq 100 Into Correction on AI Spending Worries" (Nov 2, the day before the vote): the Nasdaq-100 closed at 27,989.82, **about 10% (10.4%) below its Oct 6 record close of 31,224.47**, as investors worried that massive AI infrastructure spending was outpacing near-term cash generation.
- CNN (Oct 30), "Nasdaq suffers worst month in over a year as AI nerves roil markets": concerns about "higher interest rates, tech companies burning through cash on enormous AI spending and weaker-than-expected earnings and forecasts".
- Comparison: a lemonade stand is worth less once sales slow down. Shown as a cause→effect: sales slow, so the "worth" tag shrinks.

**Reason 3: worry about higher interest rates.**
- CNN (Oct 30): "concerns about higher interest rates" were among October's worries.
- Reuters (Nov 6): Friday's jobs report "raised interest-rate hike concerns".
- Jefferies equity strategist Jane Gibbons, quoted in Reuters coverage of the Nov 5 selloff: "We view Thursday's selloff as reactionary, not a fundamental reset, driven by a collision of concerns around AI spend, policy and inflation."
- The film says "worry about higher interest rates" and explains the textbook mechanism (safe savings pay more, so some money leaves risky stocks). That mechanism is general finance, not a claim about this week's flows. Comparison: kids leaving the candy line when the ice-cream truck shows up.

**Did the election cause it? "Partly."**
- For: Reuters and CNBC tie Thursday's drop to the election results (the data-center and regulation fears above).
- Against "the whole story": the Nasdaq-100 was already in a correction on Nov 2, before the vote (Bloomberg). October was the Nasdaq's worst month since March 2025 (CNN, Oct 30). Jefferies called it a "collision" of AI spending, policy and inflation worries.
- On screen: "Nasdaq-100, Oct 6 record → Nov 2 close (Bloomberg)". That chart is a straight line between those two closes, labelled as such; the vote marker sits on Nov 3.

## 4. Things deliberately NOT claimed
- No claim that markets "always" rise after midterms. History studies exist (for example U.S. Bank, and many outlets citing "S&P 500 higher 12 months after every midterm since 1950"), but they are averages from a small sample, and the brief allowed this only if it fitted. It was cut to keep one story.
- No prediction, no advice, no named candidates, no party blamed or praised.
- No figures from low-quality sites (such as the "$2.5T / $3.2T wiped out" headlines) are used.

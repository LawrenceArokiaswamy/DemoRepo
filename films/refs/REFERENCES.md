# Reference reels (for look, motion and storytelling; never copy characters, stories or brands)

## Ref 1: "What your mom did after your first heartbreak" (@tinyplanetlab style, 30 s, part 1)
- Look: real 3D (game-engine/Blender look), simple toy-like people: bob hair as one solid shape, dot eyes,
  tiny features, matte clothes; cosy real-scale interiors (hallway, kitchen, bedroom) with warm window/lamp light, soft shadows, wood floors.
- Motion: real body animation (walking, crouching, setting a plate down, sitting against a door, leaning over a bed, stroking hair). Camera mostly static or slow, eye level and one top-down.
- Structure: hook title on screen ("What your mom did after your first heartbreak", one word in gold), then a counter device
  top-left ("DAYS UNTIL YOU LAUGHED AGAIN 1..5") that advances each beat; one quiet small serif italic subtitle per beat
  ("she didn't knock. she just sat there." / "she left dinner by your door." / "it always came back empty." / "she let you stay home from school.").
- Pacing: cuts at 0.5, 2.8, 5.5, 12, 18, 20.8, 25 s: long holds (4-6 s) on small acts. No voice; music only (-17 LUFS).
- Why it works: tiny specific acts of love, shown not told; the counter makes you wait for the payoff.
- Part 2 (25 s): day 7 "she didn't try to fix it." (mom lies beside her in bed, girl's head drops onto her shoulder);
  day 8 at midnight mom appears in the doorway holding a whisk ("I am. she brought a whisk." = answering an unseen line);
  moonlit kitchen, girl wrapped in a blanket, mom makes pancakes, flips one onto her own head (a gag);
  day 9 "on day nine, you finally laughed." = close-up of the girl laughing with hands over mouth; mom proudly holds up a big pancake;
  ends on the two of them at the table. Cuts at 0.5, 6.5, 7.4, 10, 13.5, 15.5, 17.5, 20.5, 23.5 s.
- The arc: quiet sadness, persistence, one silly gesture breaks it, the laugh is the payoff the counter promised.
  Humour inside sadness. Close-ups only for the emotional peak (the laugh).
- Part 3 (21 s): at the table mom slides over an old photo of HER mom with a pancake on her head:
  "my mom did the same thing to me." / "she'd been exactly where you were." (girl leans her head on mom);
  empty hallway, open bedroom door, warm light: "and she'd always be there.";
  end card over the blurred hallway: the title again, a sourced stat in small caps ("Nearly 4 in 10 U.S. high schoolers felt
  persistently sad or hopeless in 2023", CDC YRBS), a gold CTA "SEND THIS TO YOUR MOM", and a question for comments
  ("What did your mom do when your heart got broken?"), then the logo. Cuts at 5.4, 10.4, 17.4 s. Whole reel about 77 s.
- Blueprint to take (in our own stories):
  1. Title hook on frame 1 (one word in gold) and the same title on the end card.
  2. A counter that promises a payoff (days/years/times) and ticks with each beat.
  3. One small, specific act per beat; one short lower-case italic line; long holds; no voice.
  4. A silly loving gesture breaks the sadness; close-up only on the emotional peak.
  5. Generational twist: the act was handed down ("my mom did the same thing to me").
  6. A quiet, empty-room shot for the last line, then end card: sourced stat + "SEND THIS TO YOUR ___" + a comment question.

# FACTS: how-chatgpt-works

Researched 4 November 2026. Each on-screen or spoken claim is listed with its source and how it is worded in the film. Nothing here states the size of OpenAI's production models, which OpenAI does not publish.

## Spoken / on-screen claims

| # | Claim in the film | Where | Source and basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "set off **billions** of calculations for every word of the answer" (headline adds "*rough estimate") | VO n1, hook headline | **Rule of thumb:** a transformer's forward pass costs about **2 × N floating-point operations per token**, where N is the number of (non-embedding) parameters. Kaplan et al. 2020, *Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361 (forward pass ≈ 2N + a small context term). The 2N-per-inference-token convention is also used by Sardana & Frankle, *Beyond Chinchilla-Optimal*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00448. For mixture-of-experts models, N is the parameters **active** per token. **Lower bound we can source:** OpenAI's own open-weight model **gpt-oss-120b** has 117B parameters with **5.1B active per token** (model card: https://huggingface.co/openai/gpt-oss-120b; paper arXiv 2508.10925), so ≈ 2 × 5.1B ≈ **10 billion operations per token**. ChatGPT's production model sizes are not published. For "billions" to be wrong, the model answering would need fewer than about 0.5 billion active parameters (2N < 1 billion), far below even OpenAI's open-weight model, so "billions" is a conservative, labelled estimate. A "calculation" here = one floating-point multiply or add. |
| 2 | Giant counter "**10,000,000,000+** calculations for ONE token · rough estimate · ≈ 2 × active parameters" | D2 (GPU hall) | Same as #1: 2 × 5.1B active parameters (gpt-oss-120b) ≈ 1.0 × 10¹⁰. The on-screen label names exactly this basis ("2 × 5.1B active params, OpenAI's open model"); the "+" signals that larger models need more. It is not a claim about the size of ChatGPT's own models. |
| 3 | "Your words get chopped into **tokens**", tag "**1 token ≈ ¾ of a word**" | VO n4, tag | OpenAI Help Center, *What are tokens and how to count them?*, https://help.openai.com/en/articles/4936856-what-are-tokens-and-how-to-count-them: tokens are pieces of words; rule of thumb for English, 1 token ≈ 4 characters ≈ ¾ of a word (100 tokens ≈ 75 words). Ratios differ for other languages. |
| 4 | Token tiles **Why · is · the · sky · blue · ?** flip to **13903 · 382 · 290 · 17307 · 9861 · 30**; answer pieces "Sun·light· scat·ters· off· air…" (note "scatters" = " scat" + "ters"); tag "real token IDs · GPT-4o tokenizer" | T1–T2, P2–P3 | Computed on 4 Nov 2026 with the `o200k_base` vocabulary (the GPT-4o tokenizer) via the Hugging Face port `Xenova/gpt-4o` (https://huggingface.co/Xenova/gpt-4o, `tokenizer.json`) and the `tokenizers` library. Newer ChatGPT models may use a different tokenizer; the tag names the one used. |
| 5 | "Each number lands on a **map of meaning**, where king sits right next to queen" (tag "embeddings") | VO n5, M1–M2 | Words/tokens are turned into vectors (embeddings) in which related words sit close together; the classic demonstration is vector("King") − vector("Man") + vector("Woman") ≈ vector("Queen"): Mikolov, Yih & Zweig 2013, *Linguistic Regularities in Continuous Space Word Representations*, NAACL-HLT, https://aclanthology.org/N13-1090/. **The 3D galaxy is an illustration:** real embeddings have thousands of dimensions (GPT-3: 12,288, Brown et al. 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165), and the cluster positions/words in the film are hand-placed, not measured. |
| 6 | "Your prompt races to a data centre. **Thousands** of chips light up." (tag "thousands of GPUs") | VO n6, D1–D2 | Qualitative. Example of scale: OpenAI/Oracle's Stargate site in Abilene, Texas was reported to be getting 64,000 Nvidia GB200 chips by the end of 2026 (Bloomberg, reported by DatacenterDynamics, https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/openai-and-oracle-to-deploy-64000-gb200-gpus-at-stargate-abilene-data-center-by-2026-report/), with later reports of a planned 400,000+ (https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/openai-and-oracle-to-deploy-450000-gb200-gpus-at-stargate-abilene-data-center/). The film says only "thousands" and never claims which data centre serves a given prompt. **The globe route (from the Toronto area to central Texas) is illustrative.** A single answer runs on a small group of GPUs within that data centre, not on all of them; "thousands of chips light up" describes the hall, not one request. |
| 7 | "Then, **attention**. Every word looks at the others, to work out what you mean." | VO n7, AT1–AT2 | Self-attention: Vaswani et al. 2017, *Attention Is All You Need*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762. Simplification: in GPT-style (decoder) models each token attends to the tokens **before** it (causal masking), across many layers and heads. The beam strengths (sky↔blue strongest) are illustrative. |
| 8 | "It doesn't look up an answer. It **guesses the next word**." / "It picks one, then guesses again, one piece at a time." | VO n8–n9, P1–P3 | OpenAI, *How ChatGPT and our foundation models are developed*, https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7842364-how-chatgpt-and-our-foundation-models-are-developed: the model "uses that understanding to predict the next most likely word when generating a response, one word at a time". (Strictly it predicts tokens; the film says "word" in the voice and "token" on screen.) **Caveat:** when ChatGPT uses its web-search tool it does fetch pages, but the reply itself is still generated token by token. |
| 9 | Bar chart odds ("Sun 48%", "light 93%", " scat 71%"…), tag "**illustrative odds**" | P2–P3 | **Illustrative**, not measured from any model; labelled on screen. |
| 10 | "There's **no answer stored inside**." (tag "no stored answers, just numbers") | VO n11, X2 | Same OpenAI article (#8): the models "do not retain copies of the sentences … they process during training; instead, the learned parameters are adjusted". The numbers on screen are illustrative weights, not real ones. |
| 11 | "It's the world's most powerful **autocomplete**" | VO n11, X3 | **An analogy, not a measurement.** Basis: next-word prediction (#8) is the same task a phone's predictive keyboard does. ChatGPT is additionally fine-tuned with human feedback to follow instructions (OpenAI, *Aligning language models to follow instructions*, https://openai.com/index/instruction-following/), so "autocomplete" describes the core mechanism, not everything about it. |
| 12 | "trained on **mountains of text** from the internet" | VO n11, X4 | OpenAI article (#8): foundation models are developed using publicly available information on the internet, information licensed from third parties, and information from users/trainers/researchers. Scale example (not in the film): GPT-3 was trained on about 300 billion tokens (Brown et al. 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165). The film says "mountains", not a number. |
| 13 | "That reply wasn't looked up. It was **predicted**." | VO n12, loop headline | Restates #8 and #10 (same caveat as #8 about web search). |

## Illustrations that are not data
- **The millisecond clock** (000 → ~980 ms, "slowed down") is an illustrative slow-motion timeline of the stages, not a measurement. Real time-to-first-word varies by model, load and network; no stage timings are claimed.
- The question/answer ("Why is the sky blue?" / "Sunlight scatters off air, and blue light scatters most.") is written for the film; the phone UI is a generic chat screen (no logos).
- Globe: land from Natural Earth 1:110m land polygons (no borders), city lights from Natural Earth 1:50m populated places (public domain). Framed on North America only.

# Facts (claim → source, checked 2026-11-08). A worldwide one-day outage is a "what if"; the anchors are real.

1. **"In 2022, twelve million Canadians lost their phones and internet. Debit cards stopped working across the country."** Rogers outage, July 8, 2022: more than 12 million users of Rogers' internet and cellular networks affected; nationwide loss of Interac debit; ATMs and other financial services also hit. Duration reported as ~15–19 hours (some customers longer). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Rogers_Communications_outage · NPR: https://www.npr.org/2022/07/08/1110500480/rogers-canada-major-internet-outage · Euronews/Reuters: https://www.euronews.com/next/2022/07/13/rogers-outage-crtc
2. **"In 2024, one bad software update cancelled over five thousand flights in a single day."** CrowdStrike faulty update, July 19, 2024: ~8.5 million Windows devices affected (Microsoft); about 5,078 flights cancelled globally that day (≈4.6% of scheduled); est. $5.4B direct losses for the Fortune 500 (Parametrix). https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366598426/Fortune-500-stands-to-lost-5bn-plus-from-CrowdStrike-incident (This was not an internet outage; the film says "one bad software update", as a scale reference.)
3. **"The bill for one day: about forty-three billion dollars."** Atlas VPN (2023), using NetBlocks' Cost of Shutdown Tool: a day without the internet worldwide would cost about $43 billion. Model estimate, not a measured loss (on screen: "model estimate · Atlas VPN / NetBlocks"). https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/report-says-a-day-without-the-internet-would-cost-43-billion
4. The hour-by-hour effects (no messages/news/maps, card declined/cash only, offices offline, airport chaos, no deliveries/rides/streaming) are the film's illustration of a worldwide outage, grounded in items 1–2.
